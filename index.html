Oxidative Stress and Neurodegeneration

Introduction

Oxidative stress occurs when reactive oxygen species (ROS) production exceeds the capacity of antioxidant defense systems. Neurodegeneration involves progressive neuronal dysfunction and loss and is characteristic of disorders including Alzheimer’s disease, Parkinson’s disease and motor neuron disease. Because neurons have high energy requirements, they depend strongly on mitochondrial function and tightly regulated redox balance.

Mechanism

Mitochondrial dysfunction and oxidative stress are closely interconnected. Mitochondria generate cellular energy through oxidative phosphorylation and are also an important intracellular source of ROS. When antioxidant defenses are overwhelmed, excessive ROS can damage DNA, proteins and lipid membranes, impair mitochondrial integrity and further increase ROS generation. In neurons, persistent oxidative damage can disrupt energy metabolism, cellular signalling and neuroinflammatory pathways, contributing to neuronal dysfunction and death.

Evidence

Recent evidence indicates that oxidative stress is dynamic and context-dependent. An initial oxidative response may activate adaptive cellular signalling, whereas persistent or excessive oxidative stress can promote cellular injury and neurodegeneration. Mitochondrial dysfunction and ROS generation can therefore form a self-reinforcing cycle. Ferroptosis, an iron-dependent form of regulated cell death associated with lipid peroxidation and impaired GPX4 activity, has also emerged as a mechanism connecting redox imbalance with cellular death.

The scientific writing approach here is deliberately mechanistic: define the biological concept, explain the pathway, interpret current evidence and then connect the evidence to therapeutic implications.

Implications

These findings suggest that therapeutic strategies may need to account for disease stage rather than treating oxidative stress as uniformly harmful. The blood–brain barrier remains a major translational challenge, while approaches such as single-cell redoxomics and spatial transcriptomics may help identify disease-specific redox biomarkers and support more personalised strategies.

Conclusion

Oxidative stress is best understood as a dynamic biological process whose effects vary with intensity, duration and cellular context. In neurodegenerative disease, an adaptive response may become detrimental when persistent or excessive, contributing to mitochondrial dysfunction, cellular damage and neuronal death.

Reference

Zhang L, Zhai X, Yan Y, Xiang L, Zhang Y, Zhou X, Cai L, Tang Z, Xia Y. Oxidative stress in neurodegeneration: from a simple insult to a dynamic regulator. Redox Report. 2026;31(1):2654906. doi:10.1080/13510002.2026.2654906.
